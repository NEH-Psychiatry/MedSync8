# Wisconsin APRN Independent Practice Requirements — 2025 Wisconsin Act 17 (APRN Modernization Act, AB 257)

Source: informational summary "Wisconsin APRN Independent Practice Requirements, Based on 2025 Act 17, Effective September 1, 2026" (5 pages; no author of record). It is a secondary summary, not the statute. For legal guidance consult the full text of 2025 Wisconsin Act 17 (docs.legis.wisconsin.gov, search "2025 Act 17") and the Wisconsin Board of Nursing (dsps.wi.gov). Public policy document; no patient information.

## Quick reference

- Signed into law August 8, 2025, by Governor Tony Evers. Effective September 1, 2026.
- Governing body: Wisconsin Board of Nursing, administered by the Department of Safety and Professional Services (DSPS).
- Recognized roles: Nurse Practitioner (NP), Certified Nurse-Midwife (CNM), Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist (CRNA), Clinical Nurse Specialist (CNS).
- Replaces the Advanced Practice Nurse Prescriber (APNP) certification with a distinct APRN license.

## Background

Before 2025 Wisconsin required all APRNs (then titled APNPs) to operate under a formal collaborative agreement with a physician or dentist in order to prescribe. Earlier independence bills were vetoed in 2022 and 2024 over qualification standards. AB 257 added more rigorous supervised-practice requirements, enhanced pain-management oversight for CRNAs, and stricter title protections, and was signed as 2025 Wisconsin Act 17.

## APRN licensure requirements

An applicant must: hold a valid Wisconsin RN license (or apply concurrently); complete an accredited graduate or postgraduate program in one of the four recognized roles; hold a current Board-approved national certification in that role; and maintain active malpractice liability insurance at all times.

Transition: any person holding a valid Wisconsin APNP certificate as of August 31, 2026 automatically receives an APRN license with the appropriate specialty designation on September 1, 2026. No separate application is required.

Renewal: every two years. Renewal requires at least 16 contact hours of continuing education per biennium in clinical pharmacology or therapeutics relevant to the practice area, including at least 2 contact hours on responsible prescribing of controlled substances, plus the biennial nursing workforce survey.

## Pathway to independent practice

Independent practice (practicing, diagnosing, treating, prescribing, and managing care without a physician or dentist collaborative agreement) requires two sequential, documented sets of supervised clinical hours:

| Requirement | Hours | Minimum duration | Prior-state hours count |
|---|---|---|---|
| RN-level clinical practice | 3,840 | 24 consecutive months | Yes |
| APRN-level practice with a physician or dentist immediately available for consultation | 3,840 | 24 months | Yes |
| Total | 7,680 | about 4 years minimum | |

RN hours completed during an accredited RN program may count; hours during an advanced-practice program do not. After both requirements and at least 24 months of APRN-level practice, the APRN may apply to the Board for independent practice authorization.

Certified Nurse-Midwives are exempt from the 3,840-hour transition-to-practice requirement and may practice independently on licensure.

## Exceptions and limitations

- Pain syndrome management: APRNs using invasive techniques to treat pain persisting (or expected to persist) more than three months must maintain a collaborative agreement with a licensed pain medicine physician regardless of independent status. This most directly affects CRNAs.
- Employer discretion: the Act removes the legal requirement for physician collaboration but expressly preserves an employer's or group practice's right to require collaborative agreements as a condition of employment.
- Prescribing restrictions: the Board of Nursing retains authority to specify drug classes, individual drugs, or devices that APRNs may not prescribe.

## Prescriptive authority

Prescriptive authority is tied directly to APRN licensure, including controlled substances subject to DEA registration and state restrictions. The separate "Certificate to Issue Prescription Orders" is eliminated. Holders of a valid prescription certificate under the old system are automatically granted prescriptive authority; APRNs who did not hold one as of January 1, 2026 carry a license notation restricting prescriptive authority until they satisfy the applicable requirements.

## Malpractice insurance and the compensation fund

All APRNs must maintain malpractice liability insurance at no less than the minimums for participation in the Injured Patients and Families Compensation Fund (IPFCF). APRNs who elect independent practice outside a collaborative or employment relationship must enroll and pay into the IPFCF (NPs, CRNAs, CNSs; CNMs under the automatic exception are not subject to it).

## Billing and payor requirements

The Act modifies Wisconsin state law only. It does not alter federal billing regulations or private payor contract requirements. APRNs and their practices must continue to comply with Medicare conditions of participation and payment rules, state Medicaid coverage and billing guidelines, and private payor contract terms. Independent practice authority under state law does not automatically entitle an APRN to bill independently under all payor arrangements; review payor enrollment and contracting obligations before discontinuing any collaborative agreement.

## Recommended action steps

Document RN-level hours (dates, facilities, hours per period, including other states); document APRN-level hours with verification that a collaborating provider was immediately available; monitor Board of Nursing rulemaking (beginning Fall 2025) for application forms, hour verification, CE and practice standards; review employment agreements for contractual collaboration requirements; confirm malpractice coverage meets IPFCF minimums and whether enrollment is required; audit payor contracts and Medicare/Medicaid enrollment for billing implications.
